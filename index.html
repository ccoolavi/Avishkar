Decision-making in practice

How I approach consequential decisions.

Decisions are not abstract leadership claims. They are visible in the alternatives considered, the constraints evaluated, the choices made and the outcomes accepted.

01

Choosing the right infrastructure rather than defaulting to a provider

Situation: A business-facing AI service needed reliable inference without making the primary application dependent on a single provider. Decision: Evaluated self-hosted and cloud alternatives against GPU resources, model compatibility, cost, performance and operational requirements, then selected OCI GPU infrastructure. Execution: Deployed an independent API-based inference service with a primary and backup provider path.

02

Redesigning the process instead of automating broken workflows

Situation: Billing and inventory operations relied on manual processing and recurring transcription corrections. Decision: Instrumented the existing process, identified structural bottlenecks and redesigned the workflow instead of simply digitising every existing step. Execution: Implemented the redesigned operating process and successive production releases, reducing manual processing time by approximately 70%.

03

Protecting enterprise delivery during platform migration

Situation: Client reporting was transitioning to NIQ's proprietary Discover platform. Decision: Prioritised configuration validation, coverage comparisons, output checks, stakeholder readiness and continuity over treating the migration as a technical cutover alone. Execution: Personally handled client configurations, data loads, output comparisons, monitoring and sign-offs. The transition concluded with zero migration-related escalations to senior management.

04

Establishing performance measures before building dashboards

Situation: Operational reporting lacked a consistent framework for management decisions. Decision: Defined the KPIs that mattered to the business — inventory movement, production throughput, vendor performance and product-line margins — before designing reporting interfaces. Execution: Established dashboards and recurring reporting around the defined operating measures.
